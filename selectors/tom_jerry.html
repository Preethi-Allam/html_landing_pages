Tom and Jerry is one of the most iconic animated cartoon series ever made. Unlike Doraemon, it doesn't depend much on dialogue—the comedy mostly comes from physical comedy, timing, expressions, and the endless rivalry between Tom and Jerry.

Who are Tom and Jerry?

Tom is a gray-and-white cat, and Jerry is a small brown mouse. Tom usually tries to catch Jerry, while Jerry tries to escape, outsmart Tom, and sometimes deliberately provoke him. The funny part is that Tom isn't necessarily evil. He's usually just doing what cats naturally do—trying to catch a mouse. But their rivalry becomes ridiculously elaborate.

The basic formula

Tom sees Jerry → Tom tries to catch Jerry → Jerry escapes → Tom gets angry → Jerry tricks Tom → Tom creates a bigger plan → everything goes completely crazy.


Sometimes Jerry starts the trouble. Sometimes Tom is simply trying to do his job. Sometimes Jerry deliberately tricks Tom because he enjoys messing with him. And sometimes they actually help each other when there's a bigger threat. So their relationship is better understood as: Rivals → enemies → friends → rivals again.

Why doesn't Tom just catch Jerry?

If Tom successfully caught Jerry permanently, the cartoon would end. The entire comedy comes from the fact that Tom is almost successful but Jerry finds a way out. And sometimes Tom actually does win



People often think Jerry is intelligent and Tom is stupid. That's not completely true. Tom can actually be very clever when he's planning something. His biggest weakness is that he becomes: angry + impatient + overconfident. Jerry understands this and uses it against him. So the real battle is often: Tom's creativity vs Jerry's cleverness
